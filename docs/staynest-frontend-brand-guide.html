01 · Brand foundation

A softer way
to find your place.

Purpose

Define StayNest's promise, audience, and character.

Why it matters

A shared identity makes every page feel like one thoughtful host.

How to apply it

Use these traits to guide copy, visual choices, and product decisions.

01

What the brand promises

We help guests find a place that fits the shape of their trip, then make the path from browsing to booking feel clear and reassuring.

Brand idea
Thoughtful stays, made easy to find.

Who we serve

City visitors, remote workers, families, couples, and longer-stay guests who value welcoming spaces, honest details, and predictable booking steps.

Character

Warm, not sugary.
Refined, not formal.

Our interface should feel like a good host: attentive, quietly confident, and generous with useful information.

  • Welcoming — personal language, never overfamiliar
  • Grounded — real places and concrete details
  • Composed — clear hierarchy, uncluttered controls
  • Trustworthy — transparent pricing and status

Messaging principles

01

Lead with the place

Name the stay, location, price, capacity, and what makes it distinctive.

02

Use human words

Prefer “Find your next stay” to generic product or software language.

03

Keep promises precise

Never call a reservation confirmed until payment and booking status are verified.

Application rules

  • Use charcoal ink for body text on the cool paper canvas.
  • Use bright berry for primary actions and broad brand surfaces.
  • Coral is an accent, not a paragraph-text color.
  • Use white for card surfaces and reverse text on bright berry.
  • Use warm red for errors and cool blue for success feedback.
Contrast note Coral and blush are accent colours, not small-text colours. Use charcoal or bright berry text on pale blush, and white text on bright berry only after checking contrast for the chosen type size.

04 · Photography & art direction

Show the real
reason to stay.

Purpose

Make the actual apartment the visual centre of discovery.

Why it matters

Honest imagery helps guests assess fit and builds booking confidence.

How to apply it

Choose clear, useful views and keep crops, quality, and alt text consistent.

04

Image direction

  • Lead with a clear, wide view of the actual room or property.
  • Prefer natural light, honest perspective, lived-in but uncluttered details.
  • Show distinct spaces: living room, bedroom, kitchen, exterior, view.
  • Use consistent quality and crop treatment across the listing collection.
  • Hero photography uses a lighter garnet wash so the room stays visible while white text remains readable.

Crop and treatment

  • Property cards: landscape crop, consistent frame, focal point remains visible.
  • Detail page: wide cover image with space to inspect the property.
  • Authentication: immersive full-height photograph with subtle dark wash.
  • Avoid heavy blur, artificial gradients over photos, or unrelated stock imagery.
  • Write useful alt text describing the scene, not repeating “image of”.

Where imagery appears

01 · Discovery

Large hero image signals the product. Cards use consistent landscape property photography.

02 · Evaluation

A prominent detail cover supports a considered decision before a guest reserves.

03 · Account

A single immersive image adds warmth to sign-in without competing with the form.

06 · Components, voice & interaction

Quiet controls.
Confident decisions.

Purpose

Make recurring controls and feedback feel consistent.

Why it matters

Familiar patterns reduce errors and make each action easier to understand.

How to apply it

Use clear labels, restrained icons, stable states, and purposeful surfaces.

06

Buttons

Find your stayCancel
  • Primary: bright berry fill, white text, concise verb-led label.
  • Secondary: outlined or text treatment; use for reversible, lower-priority actions.
  • Keep buttons stable in size and label; show busy state without shifting layout.
  • Do not append decorative arrows to every button.

Forms and feedback

  • Labels remain visible above fields; never rely on placeholder alone.
  • Use native date, numeric, and select controls when they fit the task.
  • Put validation near the field or action that needs attention.
  • Errors use restrained warm red; success uses cool blue with explicit text.
  • Show pending, confirmed, cancelled, and failed states in words.

Iconography

Use a consistent outline family (Lucide is already in the frontend). Keep icons small, aligned, and subordinate to labels. Reserve icon-only buttons for familiar actions and provide accessible names/tooltips. Remove decorative duplication from wordmarks, field rows, and amenity lists.

Surface and shape

Cards are functional containers, not decoration. Use white surfaces, fine warm-grey borders, restrained shadows, and small corner radii. Avoid nested cards and excessive pill styling.

Voice examples

Prefer Avoid
“Find your place in the world.” “The ultimate accommodation solution”
“₦85,000 per night” Unexplained totals or hidden fees
“Your reservation is confirmed.” Claiming success before verification
“No apartments match those filters.” “Oops! Nothing here!”

Motion

Motion should clarify rather than entertain: a subtle image hover, a restrained menu reveal, or a loading state. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; never make essential information depend on animation.

07 · Accessibility & implementation

Make the calm
work for everyone.

Purpose

Set a baseline for accessible, responsive product experiences.

Why it matters

Inclusive patterns work across devices, abilities, and real usage conditions.

How to apply it

Use the checklist during design, implementation, and release review.

07

Accessibility checklist

  • Maintain readable contrast for body and control text.
  • Use semantic headings, buttons, links, forms, and landmarks.
  • Give every input a programmatic label and every image useful alt text.
  • Make focus visible and keep all interactions keyboard-operable.
  • Do not communicate booking state through colour alone.
  • Respect reduced motion and zoom/reflow at 200%.
  • Keep tap targets comfortable on touch screens.

Responsive behaviour

  • Desktop: three property columns, horizontal search row, split auth.
  • Tablet: simplify navigation and reduce columns before content gets cramped.
  • Mobile: one-column listings, stacked search controls, clear form spacing.
  • Keep the property title, price, and primary reservation action easy to find.
  • Prevent long city names, prices, and buttons from overflowing.
